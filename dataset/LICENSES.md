# Licences, credits and citations for the training data

This page explains, folder by folder, where the training audio came from, who
must be credited, and what you may do with it. It is a practical summary to
help you share the data correctly, not legal advice.

## Summary

| Folder | Source | Licence | When you share it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `speech_clean/` | Edinburgh noisy-speech database (clean speech, VCTK / Voice Bank speakers), obtained through Microsoft MS-SNSD | **CC BY 4.0** | Credit the database (citation below) and say that you selected a subset and converted it to FLAC |
| `noise_defence_synthetic/` | Generated by this project's `defence_noise.m` | **This project's own work.** No third-party recordings are inside | Nothing is required. If you publish the clips, choose a licence for them (CC BY 4.0 and CC0 are common for datasets) |
| `noise_general/` | Microsoft MS-SNSD noise: Freesound (CC0) and DEMAND (CC BY-SA 3.0) | **CC BY-SA 3.0**, applied to the whole folder (see below) | Credit DEMAND and MS-SNSD, link the licence, and share any changed version under CC BY-SA 3.0 or a compatible licence |
| `example_pairs/` | Mixtures of the three folders above | **CC BY-SA 3.0** | The credits for the speech and for the everyday noise |
| `manifests/`, `scripts/`, guides | This project | Same terms as the project's code | — |

## Clean speech: `speech_clean/`

The 4,639 sentences come from the MS-SNSD repository's `clean_train` folder,
which redistributes the clean speech of:

> Valentini-Botinhao, C. (2017). *Noisy speech database for training speech
> enhancement algorithms and TTS models* [sound]. University of Edinburgh,
> School of Informatics, Centre for Speech Technology Research (CSTR).
> https://doi.org/10.7488/ds/2117

Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0),
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. You may copy, change and share
the files for any purpose, including commercially, as long as you give
credit, link the licence and indicate the changes.

**Changes we made:** we selected a subset (up to 80 sentences from each of
58 speakers) and converted the 16 kHz WAV files provided by MS-SNSD to
lossless FLAC. The audio samples are unchanged.

The speakers were recorded for the Voice Bank / VCTK corpus at the
University of Edinburgh.

## Synthetic defence noise: `noise_defence_synthetic/`

Every clip was computed by `defence_noise.m`, from random numbers and simple
signal-processing building blocks (filters, envelopes, harmonics, echoes).
No recording of a real weapon, vehicle or anything else is inside, so no
third party holds rights to these files. Section 3.2 of `README.md` shows how
to recreate any clip from its seed.

## Everyday noise: `noise_general/`

The 179 recordings are the complete `noise_train` and `noise_test` folders
of MS-SNSD. According to the MS-SNSD README, its noise comes from two
sources:

* **Freesound** (https://freesound.org): only clips released under CC0
  (public domain dedication) were selected. CC0 places no conditions on use.
* **DEMAND**, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0
  (CC BY-SA 3.0), https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/:

  > J. Thiemann, N. Ito, E. Vincent, "The Diverse Environments Multi-channel
  > Acoustic Noise Database (DEMAND): A database of multichannel environmental
  > noise recordings," *Proceedings of Meetings on Acoustics*, vol. 19, 2013.
  > Data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1227121

MS-SNSD does not record which file came from which source. The 16
five-minute files named after DEMAND recording environments (Bus, Cafe,
CafeTeria, Car, Field, Hallway, Kitchen, LivingRoom, Metro, Office, Park,
Restaurant, Square, Station, Traffic, Washing) are almost certainly from
DEMAND; the column `probable_source` in `manifests/noise_general.csv` marks
them. To be safe, **we apply CC BY-SA 3.0 to the whole folder.** In
practice:

* credit DEMAND (citation above) and MS-SNSD (below);
* link the CC BY-SA 3.0 licence;
* say that the files were converted to FLAC (samples unchanged);
* if you share a modified version of these files, or mixtures made from
  them, share it under CC BY-SA 3.0 or a compatible licence (for example
  CC BY-SA 4.0).

## Example pairs: `example_pairs/`

Each pair mixes speech from `speech_clean/` with noise from one or both noise
folders. We treat a mixture that contains a `noise_general` file as an
adaptation of CC BY-SA material, so we release all example pairs under
**CC BY-SA 3.0**,
with the credits for the Edinburgh speech database, DEMAND and MS-SNSD.
`example_pairs/pairs.csv` lists the exact source files of every pair.

## MS-SNSD

> C. K. A. Reddy, E. Beyrami, J. Pool, R. Cutler, S. Srinivasan, J. Gehrke,
> "A scalable noisy speech dataset and online subjective test framework,"
> *Proc. Interspeech*, 2019. https://github.com/microsoft/MS-SNSD

The MS-SNSD code is under the MIT licence. Its README states that the
datasets are provided "under the original terms that Microsoft received such
datasets", which are the licences listed above, and that Microsoft provides
them "as is", without warranties. We used the files of commit
`fe61c4ba0d9ac8dd7e23d719cc79f8947e1dc742`.

## Not included in this package

* **ESC-50** environmental sound clips: used only to test the trained model
  on real helicopter, fireworks, siren and engine recordings, never for
  training. Its licence (CC BY-NC 3.0) does not allow commercial use, so the
  clips are not redistributed here.
* **CMU ARCTIC** test speech and the **two recordings supplied with the
  problem statement**: they are in the GitHub repository (`audio/`), under
  their own terms, and were not used for training.
